ALBUKHR External Project Builder Guidance
Version 1.0 --- Practical Guide for Project Owners
1. Prepare Before You Open the Form
Be ready to explain: - what the project is; - who owns or controls it; - what problem it solves; - its current business stage; - exactly what funding will finance; - what evidence supports the claims; - its principal risks; - what happens if assumptions fail.
2. State the Correct Business Stage
Use the real stage: concept, setup, pre-operation, operating, expansion or mature. An operating business should explain existing operations and why additional capital is needed.
3. Define the Funding Purpose
Identify whether funding is for startup/build, expansion, working capital, replacement, branch/product growth, restructuring or a mixed purpose.
4. Build an Itemized Funding Plan
Each material cost should identify description, quantity, unit, unit cost, total, purpose and supporting evidence where appropriate.
Do not inflate costs to manufacture an artificial funding requirement.
5. Understand the Three Funding Concepts
Capital Requirement = overall project funding need.
Required Liquidity = liquidity determined or approved through ALBUKHR.
Verified Liquidity = funds actually verified through the applicable evidence/control process.
Declared assets or liquidity are not automatically verified liquidity.
6. Prepare Ownership Evidence
Prepare evidence showing who owns or controls the project, who is authorized to represent it, and any relevant ownership or related-party relationships.
7. Prepare Business Evidence
Where applicable, prepare registration documents, licences, location evidence, financial statements, management accounts, invoices, contracts, sales/production records, asset records and other credible commercial evidence.
Forecasts must be clearly presented as forecasts, not historical facts.
8. Write a Clear Project Description
Cover: Problem → Solution → Customers/Users → Operations → Economics → Capital Use → Execution Team → Risks
Avoid promotional language that hides material limitations.
9. Prepare the Team
Provide accurate names, roles, titles, contact details where appropriate, relevant experience and primary-contact status. Do not add people merely to make the team appear larger.
10. Upload Supporting Documents
The applicant workflow supports: - Business Registration; - Project Proposal; - Business Plan; - Financial Statement; - Identity Document; - Ownership Document; - Other.
Use authentic, readable files and only submit documents you have authority to provide.
11. Protect Private Information
Use the designated ALBUKHR private upload workflow. Do not expose private storage objects, share credentials, or attempt to bypass document-access controls.
12. Understand Due Diligence
Be prepared for verification of owner identity, ownership authority, registration, business identity, location, operating status, legal/regulatory dependencies, related-party conflicts and business evidence.
13. Understand Risk Review
Relevant dimensions include business model, execution, financial/economic, liquidity, operational, ownership/governance, fraud/market integrity and legal/regulatory risk.
A useful structure is:
Risk → Evidence → Mitigation → Remaining Exposure
Do not hide material risks.
14. Prepare for Funding Review
Expect ALBUKHR to compare declared funding, itemized funding, reserves, contingency, project stage, operating cycle and evidence. The approved capital amount may differ from the original request.
15. Prepare for Custody Controls
Some projects require custody, segregation, deposit verification, release authorization, release conditions, reconciliation or exception controls. Do not claim a control is complete until it has been verified.
16. Understand Terms
Terms may address investment model, asset, target capital, minimum/maximum investment, duration, funding window, return model, principal treatment, exit, fees, distributions, default and conditions.
17. Prepare Disclosure
Separate: - verified facts; - project declarations; - forecasts/assumptions.
Disclose material risks, conflicts, legal/regulatory dependencies, liquidity limitations, custody arrangements and material restrictions.
18. Understand Technical Readiness
Technical review may verify application/project binding, network alignment, registry integrity, owner binding, metadata, project logo and uniqueness.
19. Understand Approval vs Activation
The lifecycle is:
Application → Ownership Verification → Due Diligence → Risk Assessment → Funding Structure → Custody Controls Where Required → Terms → Disclosure → Funding Readiness → Technical Readiness → ACTIVE
An approved project can remain inactive until all required gates pass.
20. Common Causes of Delay
Check for: - inconsistent names; - incomplete ownership evidence; - missing registration evidence; - unexplained funding figures; - unsupported financial claims; - incomplete team; - missing documents; - unclear funding purpose; - unresolved risk factors; - incomplete custody controls; - inconsistent terms/funding; - outdated disclosure; - technical inconsistencies.
21. Never Do These
Never fabricate documents, alter financial records, exaggerate performance, claim unverified liquidity is verified, promise guaranteed returns, hide ownership relationships, submit unauthorized documents, bypass review controls, manipulate the database/API, or mix Mainnet and Testnet information.
22. Final Submission Checklist
Identity: authority and ownership are clear.
Business: identity, registration and location are accurate.
Project: purpose, model and team are clear.
Funding: costs are itemized and reasonable.
Evidence: required documents are authentic and readable.
Risk: material risks and conflicts are disclosed.
Technical: registry information is complete and consistent.
23. What Happens After Submission?
The application enters the review workflow. ALBUKHR may request evidence, perform due diligence and risk assessment, review funding, require custody controls, prepare/review terms and disclosure, verify technical readiness, and finally evaluate the authoritative activation gate.
Best practice: Prepare the evidence first. Submit the story second.
